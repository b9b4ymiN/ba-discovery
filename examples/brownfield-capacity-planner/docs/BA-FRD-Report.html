This project helps planners prioritize constrained orders consistently while preserving human authority.

1. Background

Today, constrained allocation depends on spreadsheets and personal judgment, making decisions difficult to explain consistently.

When there is not enough material for every order, the system helps make the reasoning visible instead of leaving it only in a planner’s head.

2. Goal

Provide a traceable recommendation that improves consistency without replacing planner judgment.

3. What We Will Do

Read approved order/material data, apply priority rules, explain the recommendation, and support review/override within policy.

4. Scope & Boundaries

4.1 In Scope

  • Prepare eligible orders
  • Recommend priority
  • Explain reasoning
  • Review and permitted override

4.2 Out of Scope

  • Autonomous production release
  • Replacing ERP

4.3 System / Process Boundary

The planner workflow begins with ERP-sourced order/material data and ends with an approved allocation decision.

4.4 External Responsibilities

ERP owns order/material truth; managers own confirmation of named exceptional overrides.

4.5 Non-Goals

Removing planner authority is explicitly not a goal.

5. Current → Future

Current
ERP + spreadsheet + personal judgment
Future
ERP → recommendation → planner decision

7. Functional Requirements

7.1 Prioritize constrained orders

Purpose: Make allocation priority consistent and explainable.

When It Happens: Available material cannot satisfy every eligible order.

How It Works: Evaluate approved rules, produce an ordered recommendation, show reasons, then wait for planner action.

Business Rules: Policy-driven priority; exceptional overrides require manager confirmation.

Example: Two orders compete for limited material; the recommendation explains which rule placed one ahead.

Exceptions: Missing data or a policy conflict remains visible instead of being silently resolved.

Expected Result: A reviewable proposed allocation.

9. Data & Integrations

ERP is the source of truth for order and material availability. The BA example does not define a transport protocol.

10. Roles, Responsibilities & Permissions

Planner reviews normal recommendations; manager confirms policy-defined exceptional overrides.

11. Exceptions & Edge Cases

Invalid/missing source data and unresolved policy conflicts must remain explicit and block silent finalization.

12. Non-Functional Requirements

No numeric performance target is invented in this example. The ~30-user statement remains an estimate, not a hard capacity requirement.

13. Acceptance & Success Criteria

The recommendation is reviewable, rules are explainable, planner authority is preserved, and exceptional overrides follow the approved confirmation rule.

14. Risks, Assumptions & Open Items

Priority policy may change and requires a clear owner.

~30 active planners is an unconfirmed estimate used only as contextual evidence.

No blocking open item in the synthetic approved scope.
